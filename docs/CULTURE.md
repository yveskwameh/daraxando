# Rooted in Nigeria: look, pieces, avatars, voice and name

Status: approved by Yves and built into the app. Yves approved the name (Dara X&O), the Sticker logo, the folktale avatars, the Ankara sticker buttons and the voice. Collections unlock by level: 4 of each are open to new players. The aim is a game that feels like all of Nigeria, not one city or one group, and that never turns culture into a costume.

## Principles

1. **Everyday life, not exotica.** Danfo, keke, puff-puff, suya and folktales, the things people actually grew up with. Maliyo Games built its catalogue the same way ("a reflection of what we see living in Nigeria").
2. **Every region gets the good moments.** Words, pieces and avatars come from the North, South-South, South-East and South-West, and each language gets winning lines, not only consolation lines.
3. **No faces, no skin tones, no caricature.** Avatars are animals and pieces are objects, so nobody is drawn as a stereotype.
4. **Nothing sacred.** No masquerades, deities, ritual objects or secret-society signs (list below).
5. **Original art only.** Prints are drawn from scratch in the spirit of Ankara, never traced from a real fabric.

## Prints (Ankara sticker buttons and unlockable "fabrics")

Buttons are the Danfo sticker shape (cream border, always straight, never tilted) filled with a print, a different print per button. Pressing squeezes the button, slides the print and throws sparks. On buttons the print is drawn one tone darker than the fill so white text keeps its contrast. In full colour, the same prints become each player's "fabric": the ring around their avatar and the band on their nameplate in matches. The simpler prints are open from the start and the finest ones unlock at higher levels.

Rules for prints:
- Never copy a Vlisco or GTP design, its name or its design number. Their designs are protected, and Nigeria's industrial-design carve-out does not protect us from a Dutch company.
- Never use real Nsibidi glyphs, Ukara cloth, Arabic script, or the Akwete "ikaki" tortoise motif (reserved for royalty).
- Don't name a print after a deity (for example Olokun).

## Pieces (replacing X and O)

In the app this collection is called **"X & O"**, so players instantly understand what it is: the thing you place on the board. Each player brings their own piece. Yours is always indigo and your opponent's is always pink, so colour and shape both tell them apart. If both players pick the same piece, the opponent's switches to its pair. The status line names your piece ("Oya, place your drum").

| Pair | Why it works | Unlock |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Talking drum and calabash | Hourglass against bowl. The drum is dùndún in Yoruba, kalangu in Hausa, obodom in Efik | Start |
| Danfo and keke | Long box against tall wedge. Lagos against everywhere | Start |
| Puff-puff and chin chin | Round balls against small squares, which echo O and X | Level 3 |
| Gele and fila | Fan against cap | Level 6 |
| Suya and bole | Diagonal skewer against curved plantain. The North against Port Harcourt | Level 9 |
| Zobo and kunu | Tall bottle against cup with a straw | Level 12 |

Kept out: kola nut (a ritual object), masks, and religious symbols.

Baba Ayo, the bot, is the tortoise wearing a fila, with a BOT tag. "Baba" is the respectful word for an elder or father, used across Nigeria, and "Ayo" means joy in Yoruba and is also the name of the Yoruba seed game. The button always says "Play Baba Ayo" and the sheet explains he is the game's bot. Never "practice".

Before launch, a native speaker of each language should check every animal name and line of copy.

Left out on purpose: the leopard (the Ekpe society's emblem), monkeys and gorillas (they carry a history of racist imagery), the owl (tied to witchcraft beliefs), cattle and rams (tied to conflict and religious festivals), Egungun and other masquerades, Orisha and other deities, Benin Bronzes (ownership is disputed), the national coat of arms and flag (using them in business without a licence is an offence).

## Voice: game moments

**Changed on 17 October 2026** (Yves: the Nigerian vibe was too much; cut it down without stripping it out). The approved wording, row by row, is `docs/COPY-PASS.md`. Now:

- **Match text is plain** ("Your move. Place your drum.", "Their move", "They blocked you.", "THEY WIN", "DRAW"), with **one fun line on wins**: the stamp when you win a game is one of GBAM!, ODOGWU! or E CHOKE!, picked at random.
- **Baba Ayo keeps a light Pidgin voice in his own lines only**: his chat in games against him (`worker/baba.ts`), his nudges and leave lines (`shared/nudges.ts`) and the 7 pm reminder (`shared/notify.ts`). English anyone reads at a glance, with his "o", "abeg", "Haba", "Oya", "Notin spoil", "Shikena", "Yawwa", "Odogwu" and "You too much". Words most players would have to look up (Jeje in a sentence, Jisi ike, Ndo, Gbas gbos) are gone from his lines.
- **Quick messages players send each other**: Good game, Nice one, Your move, Gbam!, Haba!, No rage bait me ("No rage bait me" is from Trench Boy's skits, viral since late September 2026; rotate it out by early 2027).
- Instructions, labels, settings and the notification "friend joined your room" are plain standard English. "Sapa relief" in the wallet is now "Free top-up".
- Unchanged: the titles, Baba Ayo's levels (Jeje, Sharp-sharp, Agba), the animals, pieces and prints with their names, places and the music.

Words still in use and what they mean: Oya (let's go), Haba (come on now, Hausa), Gbam (exactly), Yawwa (bravo, Hausa), E choke (overwhelming), Odogwu (champion, Igbo), Notin spoil (all is well), Shikena (that's it, Hausa), Shine your eye (be careful), abeg (please), "o" (emphasis). No new Yoruba, Igbo or Hausa word may be added without the native speaker's check.

Never use: insults aimed at the player (werey, mumu, olodo, ode), vulgar words (shege, waaka), political slogans (emi lo kan, obidient, soro soke, no gree for anybody), religious phrases (wallahi, God when, inshallah), "aboki" (widely heard as a slur), and phrases tied to artists in controversy.

## The name: Dara X&O (chosen)

**Dara X&O**, always with the & symbol wherever players see it. Dara is the real three-in-a-row game of the Dakarkari people of Kebbi State (Hausa speakers call it "doki"). "Dara" means good in Yoruba, rejoice in Ibibio and laugh in Hausa. "X&O" tells anyone what the game is, and matches the "X & O" pieces collection.

Web addresses and social handles can't contain "&", so they spell it out: `daraxando` (Yves checked that the spelled-out name is available). H&M (hm.com) and AT&T (att.com) work the same way.

Watch out for: an iOS app called "DARA: Old African Game" exists, and Dara is a common first name. Search the Nigerian trademark registry before launch.

The logo is option 3 in `docs/logos.html`, the Sticker: "Dara" with a fuchsia "X&O" pill sticker pressed right up against the word.

Also considered: Tic Tac Gbam (clearest, but "Tic Tac" belongs to Ferrero), XO Wahala, Oya XO, XO Arena (the old working title). Ruled out: Ẹta, Atọ and Uku ("three" in Yoruba, Igbo and Hausa), because each has an unfortunate second meaning in another dialect.
